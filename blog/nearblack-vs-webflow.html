NearBlack vs Webflow: Build It Yourself or Have It Done Right?

Webflow is a genuinely good product. Unlike Wix and Squarespace, it produces clean code, gives designers real control, and can handle sophisticated layouts. If you have the time to learn it and someone on your team who will actually use it, it is worth considering. Most businesses do not meet those conditions.

Quick verdict

Webflow: Powerful, flexible, and worth learning if you have a designer or developer in-house with 40+ hours to invest. NearBlack: Better for businesses that want a professional result without spending weeks learning a new tool. Done in 72 hours, handed over, no monthly CMS fees.

What Webflow actually is

Webflow is a visual development platform. It lets you design websites using a drag-and-drop interface, but unlike Wix or Squarespace, it exposes the underlying CSS model directly. That means you are actually learning and applying CSS concepts (flexbox, grid, positioning, animations) through a visual interface rather than writing code.

The result is that Webflow sites can look genuinely custom and perform well. But the learning curve is steep. Most users need 40 to 100+ hours of practice before they can build a professional-quality site. If you pick it up and put it down, that time resets.

Pricing starts at $23 per month and goes up to $212 per month for the CMS Business plan. Hosting is included, but like Wix and Squarespace, you are paying indefinitely and your site lives on Webflow's servers.

Where Webflow fits

Webflow is the right tool when:

  • You have a designer who will own the site and update it regularly
  • Your team wants direct control without hiring developers for every change
  • You are building a content-heavy site that needs a real CMS
  • Someone in the organization has already invested in learning it

For design agencies building client sites, Webflow is excellent. For a law firm, contractor, or service business that wants a professional website and has no interest in learning visual development, it is overkill.

The real cost of doing it yourself

Webflow looks cheap at $23 per month. But consider what you are buying: the ability to eventually build the site yourself. That "eventually" has a cost. If a business owner or team member spends 60 hours learning Webflow and building a site, at any reasonable internal rate, that is thousands of dollars of time.

NearBlack delivers a finished, professional site in 72 hours for a one-time cost starting at $999. The math is not complicated. Your time has value. Use it on your business.

The code ownership difference

Webflow does export HTML and CSS, but the CMS functionality requires Webflow's hosting. If you want to move a Webflow CMS site away from Webflow, the dynamic content does not come with you. For static sites it is fine, but for anything with a blog, a portfolio, or database-driven content, you are effectively tied to Webflow's infrastructure.

NearBlack builds sites with no platform dependency. The code is standard HTML, CSS, and whatever backend is appropriate. You can host it on any provider, forever, without paying NearBlack or anyone else a monthly fee.

Bottom line

Webflow is an excellent tool for the right person. Most business owners are not that person. If you want a professional result without a learning curve and without monthly fees, NearBlack gets it done in 72 hours.
